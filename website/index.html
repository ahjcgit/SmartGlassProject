Shared Spatial Annotations
with Smart Glasses

Experiment: November 10, 2026 · Presentation: November 12, 2026

We are building a RayNeo application that shows persistent notes for a selected object, filtered by the signed-in user. The current experiment compares two object detectors on recorded glasses-camera images.

Results

11 capture trials, 229 sampled frames, one sanitizer bottle and one toy motorcycle. YOLOX-S detected more target frames than NanoDet, but its median CPU model call took 5.02× longer.

Method and limits

Capture
480 × 640 RayNeo X3 Pro camera images. For each object: near, middle and far front views; a middle oblique view; and a slow head pan. One additional empty-scene trial. Still trials: 5 s; pans: 8 s.
Models
NanoDet Plus (416 input) and YOLOX-S (640 input), OpenCV 4.13.0 CPU, two threads. Three warmups per model, then one timed call per image. CPU model was not recorded.
Scoring
Correct category, confidence ≥ 0.35, and box intersection-over-union ≥ 0.50. Score the highest-confidence expected-category prediction. Reference boxes cover visible object extent, excluding shadows.
Annotations
18 original user boxes; 192 boxes and 19 absent labels added by AI visual review before consulting detector outputs. The AI additions are not independently human-verified; 48 small or clipped-object frames are flagged for review.
Scope
One instance per category and one trial per condition. Frames are correlated. Distance and angle are approximate. These results do not establish general accuracy, maximum range, or through-glasses alignment. YOLOX-S was evaluated offline, not in the live application.
Image-space placement measurements

In the sanitizer slow-pan trial, median top-center box-anchor error was 0.248% of the image diagonal (~1.98 px) for live NanoDet and 0.121% (~0.97 px) for replay YOLOX-S. Hit rates were 23/30 and 27/30, respectively. Errors are conditional on an available prediction; they do not measure rendered text placement or optical alignment.

Project status

The Android client captures color frames; a computer-hosted detector identifies categories; FastAPI and SQLite store and retrieve permission-filtered notes. The browser companion supports note editing. Desktop labels use image coordinates; the glasses have a minimal text-display fallback.

The November 9 hardware run documented Alice receiving three permitted notes and Bob receiving two, excluding Alice’s private note. That functional demonstration is separate from this detection evaluation.

QR UUIDs identify individual objects. Model detections identify categories and require selection of the registered instance; they cannot distinguish identical bottles. The prototype has no independent temporal tracker or persistent world anchors.

Next: verify the reference annotations, test YOLOX-S in the live pipeline, repeat trials with more objects, and measure readability and alignment through the glasses.
